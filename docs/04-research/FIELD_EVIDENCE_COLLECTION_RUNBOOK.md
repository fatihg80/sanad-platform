# Field Evidence Collection Runbook

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Active working plan

## Purpose

Turn the Evidence Collection phase into a repeatable process that can be run by the Project Manager and multidisciplinary team without relying on informal interviews or untraceable notes.

This runbook covers:

- local doctors;
- facilities;
- diaspora consultants;
- vendors/platforms;
- legal/regulatory experts;
- security/privacy experts;
- public-health/research experts.

## 1. Collection Order

Recommended order:

1. Local doctors
2. Facility clinical/administrative leads
3. Diaspora consultants
4. Clinical Governance review
5. Vendor demonstrations
6. Legal/regulatory review
7. Security/privacy review
8. Public-health/evaluation review

The order can overlap where practical.

## 2. Evidence ID Allocation

Use the ranges defined in `EVIDENCE_INTAKE_REGISTER.md`.

- F-001+ Local Doctors
- F-100+ Facilities
- F-200+ Consultants
- F-300+ Vendors
- F-400+ Legal/Regulatory Experts
- F-500+ Security/Privacy Experts
- F-600+ Public Health / Research Experts

Every collected item receives an ID before it is used in scoring.

## 3. Working Collection Targets

These are **discovery targets**, not statistically powered sample sizes.

### Local Doctors
Initial working target:
- 10–15 respondents across shortlisted facilities and services;
- avoid collecting all responses from one facility or one seniority level.

### Facilities
Initial working target:
- 3–5 candidate facilities;
- at least one clinical lead and one operational/administrative contact per facility where possible.

### Diaspora Consultants
Initial working target:
- at least 3 consultants for each serious specialty candidate before judging consultant capacity;
- include more than one country of practice where possible.

### Vendors / Platforms
Evaluate the same Sanad scenario against:
- Intelehealth;
- VSee Enterprise;
- CHT;
- Custom MVP reference option.

### Expert Reviews
Engage the expert roles listed in `EXPERT_REVIEW_REGISTER.md` before their blocking decision gates.

## 4. Interview / Form Discipline

For every response:

1. explain the purpose;
2. do not collect patient-identifiable data;
3. use the approved form;
4. distinguish exact data from estimates;
5. capture source date and role;
6. avoid leading questions;
7. record uncertainty;
8. assign evidence quality after review.

## 5. Evidence Quality Review

Before evidence becomes Validated, ask:

- Is the source direct?
- Is it current?
- Is it independently verifiable?
- Is it representative of more than one person's preference?
- Is the respondent qualified to answer the question?
- Is there a conflict of interest?
- Does another source contradict it?

## 6. Triangulation Rule

Do not close a high-impact decision from one evidence type.

Examples:

### Pilot Specialty
Should combine:
- literature/desk evidence;
- local doctor need;
- facility volume/resources;
- consultant supply;
- Clinical Governance review.

### Platform Decision
Should combine:
- official documentation;
- live demonstration;
- technical review;
- security/privacy review;
- commercial proposal;
- legal/contract review;
- exit/data portability evidence.

## 7. Specialty Decision Readiness

A specialty is ready for final scoring when:

- local need is evidenced;
- at least one viable facility exists;
- consultant capacity is evidenced;
- async suitability is clinically reviewed;
- emergency boundary is defined;
- diagnostic information is available;
- outcomes can be measured;
- no unresolved exclusion criterion exists.

## 8. Vendor Decision Readiness

A vendor/platform is ready for final scoring when:

- Sanad-specific workflow demo completed;
- Arabic/RTL status verified;
- weak-network/offline behaviour tested;
- access-control model reviewed;
- security evidence received;
- hosting/subprocessors known;
- export/exit demonstrated;
- cost/TCO received;
- legal/privacy terms reviewed.

## 9. Evidence Review Meeting

Recommended cadence during active collection:
- weekly during initial discovery;
- fortnightly once major evidence stabilises.

Agenda:

1. new evidence IDs;
2. conflicting evidence;
3. quality downgrades/upgrades;
4. decision tables affected;
5. missing expert reviews;
6. assumptions invalidated;
7. risks changed.

## 10. Decision Rule

Scores support judgment; they do not replace it.

Any decision with unresolved:
- patient-safety risk;
- legal blocker;
- privacy blocker;
- professional-practice blocker

remains Open regardless of weighted score.

## 11. Required Expert Review

This runbook should be reviewed by:

- Clinical Governance;
- Public Health / Evaluation;
- Project Management;
- Privacy/DPO for discovery data handling.

The working respondent targets may be adjusted after Public Health/Evaluation review.
