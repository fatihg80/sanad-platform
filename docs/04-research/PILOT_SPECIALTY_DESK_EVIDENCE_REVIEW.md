# Pilot Specialty Desk Evidence Review

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Preliminary desk review, not a clinical selection decision  
**Evidence date:** 2026-10-06

## 1. Purpose

Provide an initial literature-backed screen of the founding prospectus candidate specialties before field evidence is collected.

This review does **not** select the pilot specialty.

It answers only:

> Which specialties appear more or less naturally compatible with asynchronous provider-to-provider tele-expertise based on published evidence?

The final decision still requires:

- Sudan-specific clinical need;
- facility case volume;
- consultant availability;
- local diagnostic resources;
- connectivity;
- clinical governance;
- measurable outcomes.

## 2. General Evidence for the Sanad Model

Systematic reviews describe asynchronous electronic consultation as a recognised provider-to-provider model in which a primary/local clinician requests specialist opinion through a secure electronic system.

Evidence reports potential benefits including:

- improved access to specialty care;
- changes in diagnosis and treatment plans;
- referral effects;
- faster specialist decision support.

Implementation success still depends on workflow, administrative capacity, legal context, and local fit.

Sources:

- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29927711/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25995331/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31565409/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36567431/

## 3. Dermatology

### Evidence Signal

**Strong asynchronous fit.**

Teledermatology has a substantial evidence base using store-and-forward clinical history plus photographs.

A 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis reported diagnostic concordance around 76% across skin conditions and found no significant overall difference by synchronous vs store-and-forward communication type in the pooled analysis, while image quality and dermoscopy can materially affect accuracy.

Other reviews emphasise that clinician-captured images generally perform better than patient-captured images and that image acquisition training matters.

Sources:

- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41846901/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37567745/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35891983/

### Potential Sanad Advantages

- naturally compatible with asynchronous review;
- smartphone photography can be practical;
- structured history + images fit Sanad's proposed case model;
- potentially measurable management/referral effects;
- lower need for live video in many selected cases.

### Key Risks / Questions

- image quality;
- lighting/skin-tone representation;
- malignant/complex lesions;
- dermoscopy availability;
- privacy of clinical photographs;
- metadata/EXIF;
- training local doctors in image capture.

### Preliminary Desk Position

**High-priority specialty for field scoring**, subject to actual Sudan need and consultant capacity.

## 4. Radiology

### Evidence Signal

Remote image interpretation is a well-established telemedicine concept, and tele-ultrasound research in resource-limited settings suggests remotely transmitted images can support diagnosis and management.

However, radiology is not a single workflow.

X-ray, ultrasound, CT, MRI, and specialist imaging each create different requirements.

Source:

- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31552212/

### Potential Sanad Advantages

- specialist interpretation is inherently compatible with remote review;
- outcome can be clear when the question is image interpretation;
- potentially high clinical value where radiologist access is limited.

### Key Risks / Questions

- diagnostic image quality;
- DICOM availability;
- large file sizes;
- monitor/display requirements;
- bandwidth;
- local acquisition quality;
- legal responsibility for formal reports;
- whether Sanad is providing an opinion or official radiology reporting.

### Preliminary Desk Position

**Potentially strong, but technically and legally heavier than dermatology.**

Must be reviewed by a radiologist and clinical-informatics expert before scoring.

## 5. Paediatrics

### Evidence Signal

A 2024 review of telemedicine in rural/remote LMIC paediatric care reports potential for subspecialty consultation and highlights the usefulness of simpler technologies, low-bandwidth internet, and smartphones.

Source:

- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38478166/

### Potential Sanad Advantages

- clear specialist shortages may create strong value;
- broad range of subspecialty support;
- provider-to-provider advice can support local clinicians.

### Key Risks / Questions

- children can deteriorate quickly;
- age-dependent normal ranges;
- neonatal care may be high risk;
- safeguarding;
- consent/guardian considerations;
- very broad case mix;
- many cases may cross Sanad's non-emergency boundary.

### Preliminary Desk Position

**Promising but requires narrower case inclusion criteria than “general paediatrics.”**

A paediatric specialty expert must define exclusions and safe case types.

## 6. Obstetrics and Gynaecology

### Evidence Signal

Published reviews show telehealth can support obstetric and gynaecologic care, and low-income-country maternal-health literature includes provider-to-provider mobile communication.

Sources:

- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31977782/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39651021/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28283147/

### Potential Sanad Advantages

- potentially high clinical need;
- remote specialist input may support antenatal and selected non-emergency cases;
- ultrasound-related expert interpretation may be useful in some settings.

### Key Risks / Questions

- obstetric emergencies are time-critical;
- maternal/fetal risk is high;
- tele-ultrasound may require synchronous support or trained image acquisition;
- consent and safeguarding issues;
- liability sensitivity.

### Preliminary Desk Position

**Potentially high impact but high governance and emergency-boundary complexity.**

Not recommended for first-pilot selection without strong local obstetric leadership and tightly defined case scope.

## 7. General Surgery

### Evidence Signal

Systematic and scoping reviews show telemedicine use in surgical consultation, pre/postoperative care, mentoring, and follow-up, including LMIC settings.

Physical examination limitations remain an important barrier.

Sources:

- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30515013/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35428920/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34766688/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41253494/

### Potential Sanad Advantages

- remote specialist decision support may reduce unnecessary referral;
- postoperative and selected preoperative questions may fit remote review;
- educational/mentoring value.

### Key Risks / Questions

- many surgical cases require physical examination;
- emergency surgery cannot fit Sanad's response model;
- wound/photo quality;
- transfer/referral logistics;
- advice may have immediate procedural consequences.

### Preliminary Desk Position

**Potentially suitable only for a deliberately restricted case subset.**

Broad “general surgery” is likely too wide for the first pilot without tighter inclusion rules.

## 8. Internal Medicine

### Evidence Signal

Provider-to-provider eConsult has a broad evidence base across specialist care, and asynchronous telemedicine can support chronic-disease and diagnostic workflows.

Sources:

- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29927711/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27075028/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37783479/

### Potential Sanad Advantages

- likely large case volume;
- broad specialist value;
- laboratory/result interpretation works well asynchronously;
- strong educational value.

### Key Risks / Questions

- scope can become too broad;
- complex multimorbidity;
- many cases may require further tests unavailable locally;
- difficult to standardise one case template;
- outcome attribution may be harder.

### Preliminary Desk Position

**Operationally attractive but potentially too broad for a tightly controlled first pilot.**

Consider narrowing to a defined internal-medicine domain rather than “all internal medicine.”

## 10. What This Review Does Not Know

This desk review cannot establish:

- which specialty is most needed in Sudan now;
- which facilities can generate cases;
- which diaspora consultants are available;
- which investigations are available locally;
- which specialty has the strongest NGO partner;
- local referral burden;
- local legal/regulatory constraints.

Those factors can reverse the desk ranking.

## 11. Current Evidence-Collection Recommendation

For field scoring, prioritise evidence collection for:

1. Dermatology
2. A narrowly defined Internal Medicine domain
3. Paediatrics
4. Radiology, if facilities have usable imaging infrastructure

Keep OB/GYN and General Surgery in the candidate pool, but require stronger clinical-governance justification because of emergency/physical-examination dependence.

This is **not the pilot specialty decision**.
