# Founding Prospectus, Source Notes

**Source:** Sanad Founding Team Prospectus v1.0, September 2026  
**Purpose of this file:** Preserve the project's original framing separately from later product and engineering interpretation.

## Original Positioning

Sanad is described as a tele-expertise network connecting Sudanese consultants abroad with doctors inside Sudan.

The founding document presents:

- the healthcare problem;
- the proposed provider-to-provider model;
- a first pilot;
- clinical governance;
- legal and regulatory considerations;
- technology principles;
- business and funding model;
- indicative budget;
- roadmap;
- impact measures;
- risks;
- principles;
- founding-team roles.

## Problem Framing

The source argues that:

- Sudan has lost specialist workforce capacity during the war;
- many experienced Sudanese consultants now live abroad;
- junior or less-supported doctors inside Sudan manage complex cases;
- informal teleconsultation already occurs;
- current support is often informal, unpaid, undocumented, and difficult to evaluate;
- connectivity is fragile;
- Sudan lacks a binding telemedicine framework identified by the project team's landscape review.

## Proposed Service Model

The source defines Sanad as **doctor-to-doctor tele-expertise**, not doctor-to-patient telemedicine.

Proposed case flow:

1. Submit structured case
2. Route to appropriate consultant
3. Consultant provides written advice
4. Local doctor acts and reports back
5. Case closes and becomes available for audit

The local treating doctor is intended to retain clinical responsibility.

The legal meaning of that responsibility remains subject to qualified legal review.

## Service Types

### Routine case advice
Target: 24 to 48 hours.

### Priority case advice
Time-sensitive but not an emergency.  
Target: 4 to 6 hours during service hours.

### Virtual MDT
Weekly or fortnightly.

### Case-based teaching
Monthly.

The source explicitly states that Sanad is not an emergency service.

## Pilot Concept

- 6 months live operation
- approximately 3 months preparation
- 3 to 5 facilities
- 20 to 40 doctors inside Sudan
- 10 to 20 consultants abroad
- one specialty
- one state
- approximately 300 to 500 cases

Expected outputs:

- evaluation report;
- peer-reviewed paper;
- policy brief for the Federal Ministry of Health.

Selection criteria include:

## Legal and Regulatory Position

The founding document explicitly states that the legal section requires confirmation by qualified legal advisers before the pilot.

The source identifies potential engagement with:

- Sudan Medical Council;
- Federal Ministry of Health;
- state health authorities;
- Sudan Medical Specialization Board;
- partner facilities.

For consultants abroad, the source identifies issues including:

- professional indemnity;
- employment-contract compatibility;
- regulator guidance;
- UK data-protection obligations where applicable;
- sanctions screening.

## Technology Principles

The source requires a system that:

- works under weak connectivity;
- permits cases to be drafted offline and sent later;
- is mobile-first;
- handles images such as photos, ECGs, X-rays, and results;
- supports Arabic and English;
- uses encryption, role-based access, and audit logs;
- minimises sensitive data;
- is simple enough for clinicians to adopt.

## Build or Partner Position

The founding document argues that building a platform from scratch may be expensive and slow for the pilot.

It proposes evaluating adaptation of an existing secure telemedicine platform suitable for low-resource settings, with a custom platform potentially following after the model is proven.

This is the source position, not yet a final architecture decision.

## Funding Model

The source proposes organisation-funded rather than patient-funded service.

Potential revenue includes:

- grants;
- NGO contracts;
- private-facility subscriptions;
- training;
- consultancy;
- government and donor programmes.

An illustrative case model in the source assumes a partner pays £30, of which £20 goes to the consultant and £10 contributes to platform, coordination, governance, and staff costs.

The source recognises that pilot volumes would not cover total cost and that grant funding is expected to be essential initially.

## Indicative Pilot Budget

The founding document estimates a six-month pilot of approximately 400 cases at roughly:

**£37,400 to £62,480**

This is explicitly a planning estimate rather than a final budget.
