# Pilot Evaluation Protocol

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Draft for research, ethics, and clinical review

## 1. Purpose

Define how the initial Sanad pilot should be evaluated to determine whether the provider-to-provider tele-expertise model is safe, useful, feasible, and sustainable enough to continue.

This is a project evaluation framework, not a final academic research protocol.

## 2. Evaluation Questions

The pilot should answer:

1. Can doctors inside Sudan use Sanad under real connectivity conditions?
2. Can cases reach appropriate consultants within the intended response windows?
3. Does specialist advice materially influence clinical management?
4. Can the model operate safely with acceptable incident levels?
5. Do doctors and consultants find the service useful and sustainable?
6. Can outcomes and service data be captured reliably?
7. What does one completed case cost to operate?
8. Which workflow failures occur most frequently?
9. What changes are required before expansion?

## 3. Evaluation Domains

### Access

Indicators:

- number of participating facilities;
- number of participating doctors;
- number of participating consultants;
- submitted cases;
- case distribution by facility;
- case distribution by geography at an appropriately safe level.

### Timeliness

Indicators:

- median submission-to-assignment time;
- median assignment-to-acceptance time;
- median acceptance-to-advice time;
- total response time;
- percentage within Routine target;
- percentage within Priority target.

### Clinical Value

Indicators:

- percentage of cases where advice changed management;
- referral recommended;
- referral avoided where measurable;
- additional test recommended;
- follow-up requested;
- known outcome where captured.

### Safety

Indicators:

- clinical incidents;
- near misses;
- delayed advice incidents;
- wrong-routing events;
- privacy/security incidents;
- complaints;
- corrective actions.

### User Experience

Indicators:

- local-doctor satisfaction;
- consultant satisfaction;
- perceived usefulness;
- perceived documentation burden;
- connectivity burden;
- likelihood of continued participation.

### Consultant Sustainability

Indicators:

- active consultants;
- response participation;
- dropout;
- workload distribution;
- reasons for decline/unavailability.

### Operational Feasibility

Indicators:

- coordinator workload;
- reassignment rate;
- notification failures;
- support requests;
- sync/upload failures;
- fallback-channel use.

### Learning and Education

Indicators:

- MDT sessions;
- teaching sessions;
- participation;
- perceived learning value.

### Financial Sustainability

Indicators:

- cost per submitted case;
- cost per completed case;
- consultant fee cost;
- coordination cost;
- technology cost;
- proportion funded by earned revenue if any.

## 4. Study Population

Pilot users:

- local doctors at participating facilities;
- participating consultants;
- relevant coordinators and governance staff.

Patient-level evaluation data should be limited to what is necessary.

## 5. Minimum Pilot Period

The founding prospectus proposes approximately six months of live operation after preparation.

The evaluation should cover the full live-pilot period unless modified by the founding team.

## 6. Data Sources

- platform workflow data;
- outcome forms;
- incident records;
- support records;
- structured user surveys;
- selected interviews;
- financial/operational records;
- governance audit.

## 7. Quantitative Analysis

At minimum:

- counts;
- medians;
- distributions;
- target compliance;
- completion rates;
- rates by relevant facility/specialty dimensions where safe.

Avoid overinterpreting small sample sizes.

## 8. Qualitative Evaluation

Interview/survey themes may include:

- usefulness;
- trust;
- workflow burden;
- clarity of advice;
- connectivity;
- reasons for non-use;
- privacy concerns;
- consultant motivation;
- coordination burden.

## 9. Evaluation Biases

Potential biases:

- enthusiastic early adopters;
- uneven facility case volume;
- missing follow-up;
- consultant self-selection;
- connectivity excluding the most remote users;
- reporting bias in management change.

The final evaluation should acknowledge these limitations.

## 10. Ethics and Governance

Before collecting data for research/publication determine:

- whether formal ethics approval is required;
- consent requirements;
- legal basis;
- data controller roles;
- de-identification;
- publication safeguards.

Operational evaluation and academic research should not be assumed to have identical governance requirements.

## 11. Pilot Decision Output

At pilot end, produce an explicit recommendation:

- **Continue**
- **Continue with modifications**
- **Pause**
- **Stop**

The recommendation should be based on pre-defined evidence, not founder enthusiasm.

## 12. Success Interpretation

Processing 300 to 500 cases alone does not equal success.

The pilot should demonstrate:

- useful specialist support;
- acceptable safety;
- operational feasibility;
- clinician adoption;
- reliable measurement;
- credible path to sustainability.
