# Risk, Opportunity, Challenge, and Stakeholder Analysis

**Status:** Working analysis  
**Purpose:** Identify non-software factors that could determine whether Sanad succeeds or fails.

## 1. Central Observation

Sanad is not primarily a software problem.

It is an operating-model problem supported by software.

The service must align clinicians inside Sudan, consultants abroad, health facilities, coordinators, regulators, funders, legal advisers, and technology within one safe and workable model.

## 2. Principal Risks

| Area | Risk | Potential Impact | Initial Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clinical | Incorrect or delayed specialist advice | Patient harm, loss of trust, liability | Critical |
| Legal | Unclear cross-border professional liability | Service restriction or legal exposure | Critical |
| Regulation | Regulatory objection or lack of recognition | Pilot delay or suspension | High |
| Privacy | Exposure of patient or clinician information | Harm, legal exposure, reputational damage | Critical |
| Cybersecurity | Unauthorised system access or breach | Sensitive-data exposure and service disruption | Critical |
| Connectivity | Intermittent or unavailable internet | Delayed or failed case exchange | High |
| Funding | Funding ends after pilot | Service discontinuity | High |

| Workforce | Consultant dropout or low availability | Unreliable response capacity | High |
| Operations | Weak coordination or rota coverage | Routing delays and poor user experience | High |
| Sanctions / payments | Payment or partner restrictions | Blocked funds or inaccessible services | High |
| Reputation | Serious adverse event | Loss of clinician, partner, or funder confidence | High |
| Political context | Perceived alignment with a conflict actor | Threats to neutrality and safety | Critical |
| Physical safety | Exposure of clinician identity or location | Direct personal risk | Critical |

## 3. Metadata Risk

An important security concern extends beyond explicit patient names.

Potentially sensitive metadata includes:

- IP addresses;
- device identifiers;
- GPS/location information;
- image EXIF data;
- facility identifiers;
- timestamps;
- network metadata;
- file names.

In a conflict environment, apparently minor metadata may create safety risks.

This requires dedicated threat modelling and data-minimisation rules.

## 4. Key Opportunities

### National specialist-support infrastructure
If validated, Sanad could become more than a consultation application. It could form part of a national clinical-support network.

### Clinical workforce intelligence
Aggregated and properly governed data may help identify:

- specialty gaps;
- high-demand clinical areas;
- referral patterns;
- geographic access gaps;
- training needs;
- response-capacity gaps.

### Medical education
Case-based teaching and MDTs could create a structured learning network for doctors inside Sudan.

### Policy evidence
Pilot evidence may help regulators and policymakers understand how tele-expertise could be governed.

### Diaspora engagement
The model can convert informal willingness to help into a repeatable professional contribution mechanism.

### Regional applicability
If the operating model proves safe and sustainable, parts of it may be relevant to other fragile or low-resource settings. Expansion should not be assumed before validation.

## 5. Current Challenges

These are present difficulties, not merely hypothetical risks:

- weak and intermittent connectivity;
- specialist workforce displacement;
- absence of a settled local telemedicine framework;
- cross-border professional practice;
- user verification;
- safe handling of medical images and case data;
- consultant availability;
- clinician workload;
- funding;
- institutional coordination;
- operating in conflict-affected environments.

## 8. Governmental and Jurisdictional Factors

Sanad may operate across several jurisdictions simultaneously.

For example:

- patient and treating doctor in Sudan;
- consultant in the United Kingdom or Gulf;
- funder in another country;
- hosting infrastructure in another legal region.

This may create obligations or restrictions involving:

- professional licensing;
- clinical liability;
- data protection;
- cross-border data transfers;
- sanctions;
- employment obligations;
- professional indemnity;
- financial transfers.

These questions require qualified legal review and must not be resolved solely through software design.

## 9. Adoption Risk

A technically correct platform can still fail if it imposes excessive burden on clinicians.

The product must therefore treat usability as a safety and sustainability requirement.

The relevant benchmark is not visual sophistication. It is whether a clinician can safely complete the workflow under real local conditions without abandoning the platform for informal channels.

## 10. Strategic Recommendation

Do not optimise for national scale before validating the operating model.

The pilot should first prove:

1. clinical usefulness;
2. safe governance;
3. workable legal arrangements;
4. reliable specialist participation;
5. clinician adoption;
6. low-connectivity usability;
7. measurable outcomes;
8. financially understandable operations.

Technology scale should follow evidence rather than precede it.
